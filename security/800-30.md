## Self-Host Risk Assessment
> Follows NIST 800-30

<details>
<summary><b> Purpose</b></summary>
  <div align="left">
    
### Purpose
    
This risk assessment serves two primary objectives: <br> <br>
  1. To practically apply the cybersecurity frameworks, risk modeling principles,
  and vulnerability management techniques outlined in the CompTIA CySA+ Study Guide
  (authored by Mike Chapple and David Seidl). <br> <br>
  2. To formally identify gaps, evaluate active threat vectors,
  and harden the architectural profile of the self-hosted network and information systems managed by the author.
  </div>
  </details>
  
----------------------

<details>
<summary><b> Scope </b></summary>
<div align="left">


  ### Scope
  
This assessment encompasses all locally managed network segments, host infrastructure,
  and self-hosted internal applications. None of the assets within the defined boundary are
  exposed to the public internet. To maintain operational security, strict pseudonymization has
  been applied to all assets, and technical system details are minimized where possible without 
  degrading the analytical value of the report.
  <br>
The findings and risk scores detailed in this paper remain reliable until a significant modification
occurs within the infrastructure's composition, or if internal services are subsequently exposed to the
public internet via external routing or port forwarding.

As per NIST 800-300 guidelines, specific low-relevance threat sources have
been explicitly excluded from this analysis.

</div>
</details>

<details>
<summary><b> Assumptions and Constraints </b></summary>
<div align="left">

  ### Assumptions/Constaints
This risk assessment is executed within a non-enterprise,
  residential lab environment. The target architecture is managed by an IT professional with two years
  of industry experience who actively scales, maintains, and integrates new capabilities into the 
  infrastructure for local use. <br> <br>

The primary analytical resource for this assessment is the on-site system administrator. Having personally
architected, deployed, and configured the vast majority of these systems, the administrator serves as the
internal Subject Matter Expert (SME), possessing the direct host access and technical insight required to 
investigate and validate deep architectural findings.

<summary><b> Risk Assessment Methodology and Model </b></summary>
<div align="left">
  
### Risk Model
This assessment employs a Qualitative Risk Model approach, categorizing risk levels across a standard
spectrum of LOW, MEDIUM, and HIGH. The explicit criteria and thresholds for these boundaries are defined in
Table RM-1 and Table RM-2 below. A qualitative model was selected because a quantitative, cost-benefit 
analysis is structurally impractical and non-applicable within a non-commercial, residential infrastructure
environment. <br> <br>
Operationally, this report utilizes a vulnerability-oriented assessment approach. Rather than 
focusing exclusively on adversarial intent, the methodology prioritizes evaluating internal architecture, 
structural gaps, and configuration weaknesses. These identified internal vulnerabilities are then mapped
against realistic threat sources and events to determine eventual impact and overall risk scores.

 <div align="center">
   
 ### Table 2A: Defining Qualitative Risk Model (Likelihood)
| Designation | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| LOW | Asset is not easily accessed; and/or requires physical access, and/or is never or rarely targeted |
| MEDIUM | Asset can be accessed over the network; and/or is targeted frequently |
| HIGH | Asset can be accessed over the network easily; and/or is frequent target |

 ### Table 2B: Defining Qualitative Risk Model (Impact)
| Designation | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| LOW | Minimal or no disruption to services; and/or disruption of services of no value; and/or data of little to no value; and/or disruption during non-critical hours |
| MEDIUM | Disruption to cores services of substantive amount of time during core hours; and/or data of value |
| HIGH | Compromise of core services; and/or exposure of PII or protected data |

 ### Table 2C: Defining Qualitative Risk Model (Risk)
| - | **Likelihood: LOW** | **Likelihood: MEDIUM** | **Likelihood: HIGH** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Impact-HIGH** |  MEDIUM | HIGH | HIGH |
| **Impact-MEDIUM** |  LOW | MEDIUM | HIGH |
| **Impact-LOW** |  LOW |  LOW |  MEDIUM |

 ### Table 5A: Likelihood of Event Values

 ### Table 5B: Likelihood of Event
| Event | Value | Description |
| --- | --- | --- | 
| Power outage | LOW | Systems are connected to Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS). Power grid in region is stable with infrequent moments of loss.  |

| Botnet | MEDIUM | No historical precedence for this type of attack on systems. Home network and personal devices are a frequent target. |
| Cryptojacking | MEDIUM | No historical precedence for this type of attack on systems. Home network and personal devices are a frequent target. |
